# Investigation log

Chronological lab notes, kept as written. Later entries correct earlier ones (the "5 V PD contract" theory in the first entries is wrong; see the 2026-10-10 CORRECTIONS entry). The README has the current model.

2026-10-08: whole-system lag on the docked X1. Cause: after a dock reconnect (18:41) the P40w's USB-C PD contract came up at **5 V / 3 A (15 W)** even though it advertises up to 20 V. Firmware then set `intel-rapl-mmio:0` PL1 to **11 W** (MSR RAPL stays at 64 W, so `intel-rapl:0` looks fine and misleads). Cores ran 97% busy at ~405 MHz; MSR 0x64f bit 10 (PL1) active; temps only ~60 °C; BAT0 "Not charging" at 98%.

Fast check: `cat /sys/class/power_supply/ucsi-source-psy-USBC000:00*/voltage_now` (5000000 = bad, 20000000 = good) and `cat /sys/class/powercap/intel-rapl-mmio:0/constraint_0_power_limit_uw`.

Replugging at 21:39 did NOT fix it. PD still read 5 V / 3 A even though the dock now advertised 5/9/15/20 V PDOs (`/sys/class/usb_power_delivery/pd2/source-capabilities`). Idle PL1 read 20 W, and a 4-second burst hit 2.85 GHz / 39 W at 96 °C, which looked fixed. Under sustained load, though, firmware rewrote MMIO PL1 from 20 W to **11 W** about 6 s in, and the package sat at 10.9 W / ~600 MHz / 61 °C. So: not thermal. No userspace daemon writes it (thermald exits as incompatible, ppd and tlp inactive, tuned active), so it's the EC/BIOS. A short burst test is misleading; sample `constraint_0_power_limit_uw` during at least 10 s of load.

**Why:** easy to blame Edge/Chrome/VARA, which were also busy, but the real problem was the CPU being power-starved.
**How to apply:** check the PD voltage first when "laggy while docked". Replugging the dock cable is the only known way to make the EC re-evaluate. The 2026-10-08 advice "don't raise PL1, the adapter is really only 15 W" was WRONG, see 2026-10-09 below: the input is physically fine, only the EC's belief is wrong, so forcing MMIO PL1 up is physically safe (the EC rewrites it within ~3 s, so it needs a loop; the cooler tops out at ~27 W all-core at 97 °C anyway). Probably related to the TB4 flapping (Thunderbolt connector flapping seen on logout).

2026-10-09 follow-up (user asked "is this a Linux cpufreq problem? Windows never did this"): the Linux frequency stack was verified clean: intel_pstate active, governor powersave + EPP balance_performance, max_perf_pct 100, no_turbo 0, scaling_max 4.4 GHz, platform_profile balanced, tuned balanced, ppd/tlp/thermald inactive, INT3400 `current_uuid` empty (no OS-side DPTF policy, firmware runs its own). MSR 0x64F status bit 10 (PL1) was the only active limiter; PROCHOT/thermal bits clear. Decisive test: writing 64 W to `intel-rapl-mmio:0/constraint_0_power_limit_uw` as root sent all-core clocks to 2.3-3.2 GHz / 33-35 W immediately, and firmware rewrote it to 11 W about 3 s later. So the kernel just honours what the EC sets; the EC sets it because the PD contract is 5 V. Every s2idle resume this boot logs `thinkpad_acpi: undocked from hotplug port replicator` at the resume timestamp, and the 5 V contract follows a resume, so the resume path with the dock attached is the trigger. The Windows difference, if real, is most likely in PD renegotiation on resume (Modern Standby vs s2idle) and/or Intel DTT owning the PL1 policy there, not in frequency scaling.

2026-10-09 later: user objected "5 V is impossible, the battery would discharge", and they were right. Battery-as-ammeter test: 12 spinners with MMIO PL1 re-forced to 64 W every 2 s for 40 s, package drew 27-43 W (1185 mWh in 38 s, pkg temp 96-99 °C, i.e. the cooler's real ceiling is ~27 W all-core), and BAT0 energy_now stayed at 55270 mWh with voltage flat at 17.00 V and status "Not charging". A 15 W input would have pulled ~250 mWh from the pack. So the dock IS delivering >40 W; the 5 V / 3 A in `ucsi-source-psy-USBC000:002` is a stale RDO the PPM never refreshed, and the EC derates PL1 as if on a 15 W adapter from that same stale belief (11 W in balanced/low, 13 W in performance profile, sometimes 20 W; dytc_lapmode=0, skin sensors SEN1-5 all below their 65 °C passive trips, Linux cooling devices all at cur_state 0, so nothing else explains it). UCSI debugfs `command` writes are blocked by kernel lockdown (integrity), so the live contract can't be queried from Linux. The dock's PDO list under pd2/source-capabilities was empty today (was populated on 10-08). Trigger: every s2idle resume logs `thinkpad_acpi: undocked from hotplug port replicator`. Untried: `echo sink > /sys/class/typec/port1/power_role` to force a PR_SWAP renegotiation without unplugging (may reset the dock).

2026-10-10: tried the software levers. `echo source > /sys/class/typec/port1/power_role` is REJECTED by the dock ("Swap rejected", kernel logs `ucsi_acpi: possible UCSI driver bug 4`), and `echo sink` is a no-op because the port is already sink. Unbind/rebind of the UCSI driver works partially: `echo USBC000:00 > /sys/bus/platform/drivers/ucsi_acpi/unbind; sleep 2; echo USBC000:00 > .../bind` (sends PPM_RESET on re-init). ~25 s later the EC raises MMIO PL1 to **42 W** (Lenovo's real balanced AC limit) and holds it indefinitely at idle, but after ~20 s of sustained draw >~30 W it re-derates to 13 W. So the EC policy is "weak adapter: allow bursts, then clamp", still driven by the stale 5 V contract, which survives PPM reset and (10-08) a replug. `voltage_now` still reads 5 V after the reset, so the PD controller itself holds the stale RDO. Battery stayed flat through every run (drift 55290->55200 mWh across ~10 min of repeated 30-48 W bursts), so input is physically >=40 W. The chassis thermal ceiling is ~28 W all-core at 97 °C, so the practical loss from the 13 W clamp is ~2x, not 4x. fwupd: BIOS 1.56 / EC / battery / P40w-20 dock all at latest LVFS versions, nothing to apply. Candidate workaround (not built): a 1-s loop re-writing MMIO PL1 to ~28 W, or a rebind before heavy work. Real fix needs the dock/laptop PD controllers to negotiate a fresh contract: try power-cycling the dock's own PSU, the other TB4 port, or a different cable.
Lifetime of the rebind effect (2026-10-10): not a timer. At true idle (pkg ~9-10 W) the 42 W grant held 17+ min. Under 30-48 W draw it derates to 13 W after 20-35 s. It re-grants 42 W ~50 s after draw falls below ~12 W, then flaps again as soon as draw sits at the budget. Everyday desktop with Chrome + 5K display is 12-14 W package, i.e. exactly at the 13 W clamp, so in practice it stays clamped and the rebind only helps for burst work from a quiet desktop.

2026-10-10 later, CORRECTIONS. (1) The ucsi_acpi rebind "fix" was probably a coincidence: the EC re-grants 42 W ~40-50 s after package temp falls to the low 60s, and derates to 13 W after the package has sat at 97 °C for 5-35 s. That is a thermal-hysteresis loop in the EC, not a charger belief, and the rebind just happened during a cool-down. (2) `voltage_now` of the ucsi psy is a meaningless constant on this PPM: it read 5 V with the dock AND with a 135 W charger (20 V / 6.75 A PDO) plugged directly in, while PL2 bursts of 60+ W ran with the battery flat. Source PDOs under pd2/source-capabilities ARE reported correctly, only the RDO/voltage is junk. (3) Power-cycling the dock's PSU (10:02) did NOT change the 42/13 W flapping. (4) With the 135 W charger direct into port1 (dock unplugged, lid still closed): PL1 is steady and profile-dependent, no flapping: low-power 11 W, balanced 15 W, performance 17 W; all-core ~1.4-1.5 GHz at 74 °C; PL2 bursts to 63 W allowed. So the dock changes the EC's policy (42/13 flapping, sometimes 20/11) vs a plain charger (steady 15-17 W). The 5 V/PD-belief story is dead; what remains unexplained is why the EC's dock policy flaps while the charger policy is steady. Untested: lid open (clamshell derating), the laptop's other TB4 port.

2026-10-10 10:30, after a clean reboot (docked, lid closed, performance profile): NOT improved. Idle grants 42 W. All-core load: package hits 96-99 °C within 1 s at 35-46 W, EC rewrites MMIO PL1 to 13 W after ~10-15 s at 97 °C, clocks fall to 600-850 MHz, and it holds 13 W all the way down to 62 °C; re-grants 42 W ~18 s after load stops at ~69 °C. Same thermal-hysteresis loop; reboot changes nothing. Two clean runs, both reproduced.

2026-10-10 11:00, USERSPACE WORKAROUND FOUND. Kernel is lockdown=integrity + Secure Boot, so `throttled` (erpalma) and `intel-undervolt` are useless here (they write MSR 0x610 / MCHBAR via /dev/cpu/msr and /dev/mem, both blocked). The powercap sysfs path IS writable as root. Two tests under 60 s all-core load: (a) 1 s loop writing 28 W to intel-rapl-mmio:0 → steady 28 W, 1.9 GHz, 89-95 °C, EC never derated. (b) one write of 28 W to the MSR domain `intel-rapl:0/constraint_0_power_limit_uw` (EC never touches MSR; effective = min(MSR, MMIO)) → 29-30 W, 1.9-2.0 GHz, 96-97 °C for 48 s, EC still never derated to 13 W. So the EC derate trigger is sustained draw above ~30 W, not temperature alone (it sat at 97 °C for 48 s). Caveat: in test (b) the EC had not re-granted 42 W on MMIO (it stayed at the 28 W my loop left), so EC-vs-MSR interaction with MMIO flapping is only argued, not observed. 26 W is probably a safer setpoint than 28 (97 °C is marginal). PL2 (constraint_1) left at 64 W so bursts still work. Left MSR PL1 at 28 W for the rest of this boot (not persisted). Persisting = systemd oneshot (also after resume; intel_rapl restores limits on resume itself) writing intel-rapl:0/constraint_0_power_limit_uw. thermald (installed, exits incompatible) could in theory do the same via a custom thermal-conf.xml, untested. turbostat (kernel-tools, installed) is the right monitor.

2026-10-10 11:20, test (c) MSR 28 W + MMIO set to 42 W by hand: EC derated MMIO to 13 W at 8 s (package 97 °C, draw 36-44 W), so the "derate trigger is power not temp" line above is WRONG. Better model: the EC acts only on its own two values. Register reads 42 -> derate to 13 when hot; register reads 13 -> re-grant 42 when cool and idle; register reads anything else (28 from my loop) -> EC leaves it alone (tests a and b: 48 s at 97 °C, no action). Also the CPU does NOT simply take min(MSR, MMIO): with MSR 28 + MMIO 13 the draw stayed 29-33 W for 19 s before dropping to 12 W, whereas MSR 64 + MMIO 13 dropped within 3 s. Mechanism unclear. Key practical finding: the first 10 s burst profile is identical under EC 42 W and under a 28 W cap (46/44/40/39/37/38/36/35/34/34 vs 45/43/40/39/38/37/35/34/34/33 W) because it is thermally bound at 97 °C either way, so a 28 W MMIO cap loses nothing in burst and keeps 28 W / 1.9 GHz sustained instead of 13 W / 0.7 GHz. Staged (NOT installed) service in ~/ai/pl1-cap: 1 s loop re-asserting MMIO PL1 = 28 W on AC only, install.sh / uninstall.sh. MSR PL1 restored to 64 W; MMIO left at 28 W for this boot. Lock bits (bit 63 of MSR 0x610 and MCHBAR RAPL_LIMIT) would need a kernel module under lockdown, lock until reboot and lock out our own changes: rejected.

2026-10-10 (Teams call in Edge lagging the other browser windows): the 13 W EC PL1 also starves the **GPU**. GuC SLPC's balancer (debugfs `gt0/uc/guc_pc`: "Balancer enabled / DCC in use: yes") clamps the xe GT to min=max=300 MHz, and `tile0/gt0/freq0/throttle/reasons` reads `pl1`. Writes to `freq0/max_freq` return rc=0 but read back 300, so it looks like the 90-xe-gt-min-freq udev rule got lost. It didn't; GuC overrides it. gnome-shell alone used 30-42% of rcs at 300 MHz, so a call (camera capture, VA-API encode on vcs, several decodes, compositing) saturates the GPU, and every Edge/Chrome window shares that GPU. Forcing MMIO PL1 to 28 W gave back 700/1300 MHz and `reasons=none` within 1.5 s. Fast check for "GPU laggy": `cat /sys/class/drm/card0/device/tile0/gt0/freq0/{max_freq,throttle/reasons}`. The 14:48 GT reset that day was a coincidence.

2026-10-10 later: the user bumped MMIO PL1 to 35 W, then to 42 W "to observe". Over 90 s at 42 W during a Teams call (load avg ~4), the EC did NOT derate it to 13 W, even with the package peaking at 97-99 °C. So the "42 -> 13 when hot" trigger needs more than brief spikes, probably sustained 97 °C. The package drew 23-25 W (one 31 W blip) at 2.0-2.9 GHz with the GPU at 1300 MHz max and no throttle reason, so the limit wasn't binding: the draw is set by demand plus heat, and 28/35/42 W behave the same for this workload.

2026-10-10 18:15: INSTALLED the pl1-cap service (`~/ai/pl1-cap`, `/usr/local/bin/pl1-cap`, `pl1-cap.service`). It maps the GNOME power mode (read from `/etc/tuned/ppd_base_profile`) to an MMIO PL1 on AC: power-saver 11 W, balanced 28 W, performance 42 W. It checks once a second, and on battery it writes 13 W once to hand control back to the EC. `powerprofilesctl` isn't installed (tuned-ppd); switch modes via `gdbus ... org.freedesktop.UPower.PowerProfiles ActiveProfile`. First observation from the service log: **the EC rewrites MMIO PL1 itself on a platform_profile change**, even over our value. Going to balanced it wrote 15 W three times within 3 s; going to low-power and to performance the register already read 11 W and 42 W when the loop checked, so the EC wrote those itself. Firmware defaults in that state were therefore low-power 11 / balanced 15 / performance 42 W (docked; the PD belief wasn't checked). Still open: whether a 13 W we write ourselves gets re-granted, the 42 -> 13 trigger, and the other registers.
